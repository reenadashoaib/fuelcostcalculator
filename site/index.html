How the fuel cost is worked out

The calculator finds how much fuel the trip burns, then multiplies that by the price you pay for each unit of fuel.

Fuel cost = distance × fuel used per unit of distance × fuel price

If your economy is in litres per 100 km, a 250 km trip in a car that uses 7.5 L/100 km burns 250 × 7.5 ÷ 100 = 18.75 litres. At 1.75 a litre, that's 32.81.

If your economy is in miles per gallon, divide the distance by the MPG to get gallons: a 150 mile trip at 28 MPG uses 150 ÷ 28 = 5.36 gallons. At 3.50 a gallon, that's 18.75.

US and UK gallons are different sizes

A US gallon is 3.785 litres and a UK (imperial) gallon is 4.546 litres. That's why the same car shows a higher MPG figure in the UK than in the US. Pick the gallon that matches where your MPG figure came from.

Ways to spend less on fuel

  • Keep tyres at the pressure on the sticker inside the driver's door. Under-inflated tyres raise fuel use.
  • Hold a steady speed. Hard acceleration and braking burn far more fuel than smooth driving.
  • Slow down on the motorway. Most cars use noticeably more fuel above about 100 km/h (60 mph).
  • Take off roof boxes and racks when you're not using them, and empty heavy items from the boot.
  • Compare prices before you fill up. Stations near motorways and city centres usually charge more.

Questions people ask

How do I find my car's fuel economy?

Most cars show average economy on the trip computer. You can also fill the tank, reset the trip meter, drive normally, then fill up again: divide the distance by the fuel you added.

Why is my real cost higher than the calculator says?

Official economy figures come from lab tests. Traffic, air conditioning, short trips, cold weather, hills and heavy loads all increase fuel use, often by 10–20%.

How do I split fuel costs on a road trip?

Enter the number of people sharing the cost. The calculator divides the total equally. Tick round trip if everyone is coming back in the same car.

How is the CO₂ figure calculated?

Burning a litre of petrol releases about 2.31 kg of CO₂ and a litre of diesel about 2.68 kg. The calculator multiplies those figures by the fuel the trip uses.

Do you store what I type?

No. The calculation runs in your browser and nothing you enter is sent to us.
